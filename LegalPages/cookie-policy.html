(However, please note that disabling essential cookies through your browser may affect the functionality of the site, as described below.) What Are Cookies? Cookies are small text files that websites place on your device (computer, smartphone, tablet) when you visit. They are widely used to make websites work more efficiently, as well as to provide information to the site owners. Cookies can serve many purposes, such as remembering user preferences, enabling certain features, or analyzing how people use the site. There are different types of cookies: Session cookies: Temporary cookies that remain on your device only while you are on our website and are deleted when you close your browser. Persistent cookies: Cookies that remain on your device for a set period or until you delete them. They remember your preferences or actions across multiple sites or sessions. First-party cookies: Cookies set by the website you are visiting (in this case, oxodonia.co.uk). Third-party cookies: Cookies set by a domain other than the one you are visiting (for example, a cookie set by an embedded Google Map on our site). Cookies can also be categorized by their purpose: Strictly Necessary Cookies: These are essential for the website to function properly. Without them, certain services or features (like security measures or login sessions) may not work. Preferences/Functional Cookies: These remember your preferences (like language or region) to enhance your experience. Analytics/Performance Cookies: These collect data about how visitors use a site (e.g., which pages are visited often) to improve how the website works. Marketing/Advertising Cookies: These are used to track visitors across websites to display relevant ads or measure ad campaign effectiveness. Managing Cookies Since our website only uses essential cookies that are needed for it to work, there is no option on our site to disable them without affecting site functionality. For example, if you were to disable our essential security cookies, certain pages might not display correctly or forms might not function. If you choose to block all cookies via your browser settings, you can still browse much of our site’s informational content, but some interactive features (like contact forms or any login/secure areas) may not work properly. Here are some general ways you can manage cookies on your end: Browser Settings: Most web browsers allow you to control cookies through their settings preferences. You can typically set your browser to refuse cookies, delete cookies, or alert you when cookies are being sent. Check your browser’s help section for instructions on how to manage cookies. Clearing Cookies: You can clear existing cookies from your browser at any time. Keep in mind that clearing cookies will remove cookies from all sites you’ve visited, which might remove preferences you’ve set on other websites. Do Not Track (DNT): Some browsers have a “Do Not Track” feature that, when enabled, sends a signal to websites requesting that your browsing not be tracked. While there is currently no industry standard for DNT signals, we treat them with respect since we are not doing any tracking beyond necessary operations.
