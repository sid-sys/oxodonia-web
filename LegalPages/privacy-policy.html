Oxodonia Ltd – Accessibility Policy Oxodonia Ltd is committed to making our website and online services accessible to all users, including people with disabilities. We believe everyone should have equal access to information and a seamless user experience. This Accessibility Policy (sometimes also referred to as our "Accessibility Statement") outlines our commitments to accessibility, the standards we aim to meet, the measures we have taken, and how you can contact us with any issues or feedback. Our Accessibility Commitment and Standards We strive to ensure our website conforms to widely recognized accessibility standards and meets relevant legal requirements. In particular, we are aligned with the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at the AA level, which is the internationally recognized benchmark for web accessibility. WCAG 2.1 AA includes criteria to make web content more perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust for users with disabilities. By adhering to these guidelines, we aim to provide a website that is usable by as many people as possible, regardless of any impairments or assistive technologies used. Furthermore, we are mindful of the UK’s Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) Accessibility Regulations 2018, which, while directly applicable to public sector organizations, set an important standard for accessibility in the UK. These regulations require public sector websites and apps to meet WCAG 2.1 AA standards and to publish an accessibility statement. Oxodonia Ltd, as a private company, is voluntarily committed to these high standards as a reflection of our dedication to inclusivity and best practices. We view accessibility as a continuous journey and an ethical obligation, not just a legal requirement. Our goal is that any visitor, including those with vision, hearing, motor, or cognitive disabilities, can efficiently access the content and features on www.oxodonia.co.uk. We design and develop our website with accessibility in mind from the start, and we regularly evaluate it to address any issues. 9 Accessibility Features and Measures Implemented We have incorporated a number of specific features and technical measures to enhance the accessibility of our website. These include: Semantic HTML and Structured Content: We use valid, semantic HTML5 markup for all content and interface components. Headings, lists, and landmarks are used in a logical order to create a clear hierarchy and consistent page structure, which aids navigation for users of assistive technologies like screen readers. For example, we ensure that headings (H1, H2, H3, etc.) are used to outline page content, and ARIA landmarks/roles (such as navigation, main content, footer) are implemented where appropriate. This helps screen reader users and those who navigate via keyboard to understand the layout and jump to relevant sections easily. Keyboard Navigation: Our website can be fully navigated using a keyboard alone, without the need for a mouse. All interactive elements (such as menus, links, buttons, form fields, and controls) are accessible via keyboard input in a logical order (using the Tab key, arrow keys, Enter/Space to activate, etc.). We have ensured that no functionality is keyboard-trap (users can tab into and out of components freely). Dropdown menus, modal dialogs, and other interactive components are designed to be operable via keyboard. We also provide a visible keyboard focus indicator (a clear visual highlight) on links and controls, so users can easily see which element is currently focused. This assists users with motor disabilities or those who prefer keyboard navigation. Text Equivalents for Non-Text Content (Alt Text): All informative images and graphics on our site include descriptive alternative text (alt attributes) so that users who cannot see images (for example, visually impaired users using screen readers) can understand their content and purpose. Decorative images (that don’t convey information) have empty alt attributes or are handled via CSS so they are ignored by assistive tech. We also provide captions and/or transcripts for pre-recorded multimedia content like videos or audio clips, should any be present, to ensure users with hearing impairments can access that information. (As of now, our site primarily contains text and images; if video/audio content is added, we commit to including captions, transcripts, or audio descriptions as needed.) Color and Contrast: We have chosen our website’s color scheme and text styling to ensure sufficient contrast between text (or important graphical elements) and background colors, in line with WCAG 2.1 AA contrast requirements. This helps users with low vision or color vision deficiencies read our content more easily. For example, body text and important UI text meet at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio against their background. We avoid using color as the sole means of conveying information — whenever color is used to indicate something (such as links or alerts), there is an additional indicator (like underline for links, icons or text labels for status messages) so that users who cannot perceive color differences are not at a disadvantage. Responsive Design and Text Resizing: Our website is built to be mobile-friendly and uses responsive design techniques so content reflows and adapts to different screen sizes without loss of functionality. Users can zoom in or resize text up to at least 200% on their browsers without breaking the layout or losing content*. This benefits users with low vision who may use browser zoom or custom style sheets to enlarge content. We also ensure that line heights, spacing, and container 10 widths allow for readable enlarged text. There is no horizontal scrolling required at 200% zoom on a desktop viewport for our core content. Accessible Forms and Inputs: Any forms on our site (e.g., contact forms or signup forms) are designed for accessibility. Form fields have clear labels (visibly associated with the fields) or aria-labels where a visual label is not present. We provide helpful instructions and use validation messages that are descriptive and announced to assistive tech. We avoid using time limits on form inputs. Where a time limit is necessary (rare cases), we will provide a way to extend or adjust it. Assistive Technology Compatibility: We have tested our site with common assistive technologies (such as screen readers like NVDA/JAWS and VoiceOver, and browser accessibility features) to ensure it is usable. The site’s code follows best practices for ARIA (Accessible Rich Internet Applications) markup when needed, to enhance the information conveyed to assistive devices. For example, we add ARIA roles and states to complex components like menus or dialog boxes so that screen reader users are informed about their behavior (open, closed, expanded, etc.). Our goal is that the website content is robust and can be interpreted reliably by a wide range of user agents and assistive technologies. This means as standards evolve, we remain attentive to maintaining compatibility. Skip Navigation: We provide a “Skip to main content” link at the top of the page (which becomes visible when focused) to allow keyboard and screen reader users to bypass repetitive page headers or navigation menus and jump straight to the main content. This improves efficiency for those using keyboard controls. Avoiding Flashes and Animations: We avoid using content that flashes rapidly or in high intensity, which can trigger seizures (per the three flashes or below threshold in WCAG). Our site currently does not use any such flashing content. We also minimize the use of animations or parallax effects. If any animation is present (for aesthetic enhancement), it will be subtle, and we offer controls or preferences to disable it if needed, to accommodate users with motion sensitivities. We follow guidance to avoid designing content in a way that is known to cause discomfort or accessibility issues. Consistent Navigation and Feedback: We maintain a consistent navigation structure across the site so that menus and links are in predictable places. This helps users (especially those with cognitive disabilities) learn the interface. We also ensure that interactive elements provide appropriate feedback: for example, links and buttons change appearance when focused or hovered, form fields report errors in text that can be programmatically determined, and so on. The above list is not exhaustive, but it highlights some key measures. Our development process involves using tools and manual testing to check many of the WCAG criteria, including but not limited to: ensuring all pages have descriptive titles, form elements have labels, pages are structured with headings, link text is meaningful out of context, no keyboard traps, sufficient time for tasks, no content that might induce seizures, and that help or instructions are available as needed. 11 Compatibility with Browsers and Assistive Technologies We aim for our site to work well on the most commonly used browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge, etc.) and on a variety of devices (desktops, laptops, tablets, and smartphones). Alternative input devices: including switch controls or other assistive switches, which generally rely on proper focus order and semantics.
